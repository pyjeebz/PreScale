## Launch Guard

Pick a target and a launch in the dashboard's **Launch Guard** and get a clear call:

| Call | Means |
|---|---|
| **GO** | Even the low end of the confidence band clears the launch's peak |
| **RISKY** | Only the average clears it; the band dips below |
| **NO-GO** | It doesn't clear the peak |
| **UNTESTED** | The run never ramped as high as the peak |

A checklist backs the call: capacity vs the peak, whether you tested that high, how
confident the measurement is, what breaks first and how to fix it, HTTP hygiene, and
ownership.

Test exactly for a launch in one command:

```bash
prescale investigate https://staging.myapp.com --profile product-hunt --audit --i-own-this --upload
```

`--profile` ramps to 1.5× the launch's peak so you can see your headroom. `--audit` adds the
load-free HTTP checks (compression, caching, CDN, HTTP/2) to the same run.

| Profile | Peak |
|---|---|
| `steady-10k-dau` | ~25 users |
| `product-hunt` | ~100 users |
| `reddit` | ~300 users |
| `hn-frontpage` | ~300 users |
| `black-friday` | ~500 users |

## Sign-off

On a **verified** target, sign off a run from its page, or sign off a GO from Launch Guard
for that launch. PreScale publishes a page stating the capacity (or the launch it's ready
for), the commit, who signed it off and when.

A sign-off is your statement as the verified owner: PreScale verifies that you control the
domain, and the measurement comes from PreScale's open-source CLI on your own
infrastructure. The sign-off page says exactly that. You can revoke it at any time.

## README badge

After your first sign-off, the target page shows a badge and the Markdown to paste into
your README. It keeps itself honest:

- **Green** while the sign-off holds.
- **Red** if capacity drops, for certain, below the signed-off level.
- **Gray** 30 days after the sign-off, or once 5 newer runs land without a new sign-off.

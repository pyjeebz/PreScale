A load test sends far more traffic than a normal visitor, so some hosts only allow one when
you've arranged it with them first. PreScale respects that: it recognises these hosts from
the first response and sends no load until you confirm you have permission.

| Host | Their rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Vercel** | Load testing is only permitted on Enterprise plans, with prior notice to Vercel. | [Vercel's load-testing policy](https://vercel.com/kb/guide/what-s-vercel-s-policy-regarding-load-testing-deployments) |
| **Netlify** | No load testing without Netlify's express permission, arranged in advance. | [Netlify's acceptable use policy](https://www.netlify.com/legal/acceptable-use-policy/) |
| **Cloudflare** | Load tests are treated as attacks and blocked unless arranged with Cloudflare first. | [Cloudflare on scans and tests](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/reference/scans-penetration/) |

Other platforms have their own terms. Most allow you to load-test resources you own within
their acceptable-use limits, but check yours before you start.

## How PreScale checks

Before any load, PreScale makes **one normal request**, the same as opening the page in a
browser, and reads the response headers:

- Vercel: `x-vercel-id`, or `server: Vercel`
- Netlify: `x-nf-request-id`, or a `server` header starting with `Netlify`
- Cloudflare: `cf-ray`, or `server: cloudflare`

A site behind more than one (Vercel behind Cloudflare, say) is matched to the platform
closest to your app.

## What happens next

| Where | Without permission | With permission |
|---|---|---|
| `run` / `investigate` in a terminal | Explains the rule and asks. The default answer is no. | `--host-permission` skips the question |
| `--json` and scripts | Stops with the rule and exits 1 | `--host-permission`, or `PRESCALE_HOST_PERMISSION=1` |
| `prescale ci` and the [GitHub Action](/guides/github-action) | Skips the deploy, says why on the pull request and in the job summary, and passes | `host-permission: true` |
| The [MCP server](/guides/mcp-agents) | Refuses | Start the server with `PRESCALE_HOST_PERMISSION=1` |

Only pass `--host-permission` if you really have the host's permission. It's your statement,
and the host's terms still apply.

## What to do instead

- **Test your app, not your host.** [Replica mode](/guides/replica-mode) starts your app's
  own build on your machine or in CI and tests it on localhost. What usually breaks under
  load is your code and database, and you'll find that without involving your host at all.
- **Use staging you're allowed to load:** your own servers, or a host that permits load
  testing.
- **Arrange it.** On Vercel Enterprise, give Vercel notice first; on Netlify and Cloudflare,
  ask them.

## Why this exists

We load-tested our own site, prescale.dev, from one laptop. Vercel's DDoS protection slowed
the test, then blocked our IP, and PreScale 1.0.0 blamed a "concurrency ceiling" in a site
with no workers. Then we read Vercel's policy. Since 1.0.1, PreScale reports a CDN's
firewall blocks and cached responses for what they are, and since 1.0.2 it checks the host's
rules first.
